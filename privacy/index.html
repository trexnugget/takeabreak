TakeABreak – Privacy Policy

App: TakeABreak: Stop Doomscrolling (Android) Effective date: 28 October 2026 Developer: Anup Agarwal Contact: anupagarwal199@gmail.com

The short version

TakeABreak does not collect, upload, sell or share any of your data. Everything the app knows about your phone use stays on your phone. The app's internet permission is used only by Google Play Billing, if you choose to make an optional purchase; your usage data is never sent anywhere. There is no account, no ads and no analytics.

1. What the app looks at, and why

TakeABreak helps you notice and cut down on long scrolling sessions, and gives you a focus timer. To do that it needs a few permissions. Each one is used only on your phone.

Usage access. Lets the app see which app is open on screen and for how long, and how many times you unlock your phone. This is how it counts your screen time, sends nudges when a session runs long, and shows your daily and weekly patterns.

List of installed apps. When you add an app to watch, TakeABreak shows the apps on your phone that you can open from the home screen, so you can pick from them. It reads their names and icons only.

Notifications. Used for nudges, the "Halo is keeping score" status notification, the focus timer countdown and chime, the Sunday recap and the Book Save reminder.

Display over other apps (optional). Only needed if you choose the "Full screen" reminder for an app, so the reminder can appear on top of the app you're scrolling.

Do Not Disturb access (optional). Only used if you turn on "Silence notifications while focusing" in the Focus tab. During a focus round the app switches Do Not Disturb to Priority mode, and puts your previous setting back on breaks and when the session ends. It doesn't read your notifications.

Battery and start-up. Lets the app keep running in the background, keep the focus timer on time with the screen off, and start again after your phone restarts, so nudges stay reliable. The app doesn't use this for anything else.

2. What is stored, and where

The app saves the following in its own private storage on your phone:

  • the apps you chose to watch, and the limits you set
  • daily totals of screen time, and your wins, losses and streaks (kept for 60 days)
  • your settings (reminder style, theme, Halo's guard hours, focus and break lengths and so on)
  • Book Save records, and the reading timer while it's running
  • your focus sessions: when they started, how long you focused and how many rounds (kept for 60 days)

None of this leaves your phone. Android's cloud backup is switched off for the app. If you use Settings → Your data → Back up, the app writes a file to a place you choose with the system file picker; that file is yours, and the app never uploads it.

3. What we don't do

  • We don't collect personal information such as your name, email, contacts, location, photos, messages or browsing history.
  • We don't see what you do inside other apps. The app only knows which app is open and for how long, not what's on the screen.
  • We don't use analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and no crash-reporting SDK. If the app crashes, Android may send an anonymous crash report to Google, which the developer sees in Google Play Console (you can turn this off in your phone's settings).
  • We don't sell or share data with anyone, because we never have it.

4. Optional purchases

"Buy me a coffee" purchases are handled entirely by Google Play. The developer doesn't receive your payment details. The app only learns which support tier you bought, so it can show your supporter badge.

5. Deleting your data

To delete everything the app has stored, go to Settings → Apps → TakeABreak → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app. Either one removes it all permanently. You can also take back any permission at any time in your phone's settings.

6. Children

TakeABreak is meant for adults and isn't aimed at children. Because it collects no personal data, it doesn't collect any from children either.

7. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this same address with a new effective date. If the app ever starts handling data differently, you'll be told in the app before that happens.
