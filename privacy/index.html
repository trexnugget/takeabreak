TakeABreak – Privacy Policy

App: TakeABreak: Stop Doomscrolling (Android) Effective date: 8 November 2026 (app version 4.23) Developer: Anup Agarwal Contact: anupagarwal199@gmail.com

The short version

Everything the app knows about how you use your phone stays on your phone: which apps you open, when, and for how long is never uploaded. There are no ads, and nothing is sold.

To keep the app working well, it sends anonymous app statistics (for example, that a nudge was shown) and crash reports, and it can receive news from the developer. These never include your screen time or which apps you use.

The one exception is optional. If you join the leaderboard (the Ranks tab) by signing in with Google, the app uploads your first name and your total screen time for the week, so you can compare with friends and other users. Nothing is uploaded unless you choose to join, and you can leave or delete your leaderboard account at any time.

1. What the app looks at, and why

TakeABreak helps you notice and cut down on long scrolling sessions, and gives you a focus timer. To do that it needs a few permissions. Each one is used only on your phone.

Usage access. Lets the app see which app is open on screen and for how long, and how many times you unlock your phone. This is how it counts your screen time, sends nudges when a session runs long, and shows your daily and weekly patterns.

List of installed apps. When you add an app to watch, TakeABreak shows the apps on your phone that you can open from the home screen, so you can pick from them. It reads their names and icons only.

Notifications. Used for nudges, the "Halo is keeping score" status notification, the focus timer countdown and chime, the Sunday recap, the Book Save reminder, and occasional news from the developer.

Display over other apps (optional). Only needed if you choose the "Full screen" reminder for an app, so the reminder can appear on top of the app you're scrolling.

Do Not Disturb access (optional). Only used if you turn on "Silence notifications while focusing" in the Focus tab. During a focus round the app switches Do Not Disturb to Priority mode, and puts your previous setting back on breaks and when the session ends. It doesn't read your notifications.

Battery and start-up. Lets the app keep running in the background, keep the focus timer on time with the screen off, and start again after your phone restarts, so nudges stay reliable. The app doesn't use this for anything else.

Internet. Used for app statistics, crash reports and news from the developer (section 4), by Google Play Billing if you choose to make an optional purchase, and by the leaderboard if you choose to join it.

2. What is stored on your phone

The app saves the following in its own private storage on your phone:

  • the apps you chose to watch, and the limits you set
  • daily totals of screen time per app, and your wins, losses and streaks
  • your answers to check-ins ("Why did you open it?") and your wake-up time
  • your settings (reminder style, theme, Halo's guard hours, focus and break lengths and so on)
  • Book Save records, and the reading timer while it's running
  • your focus sessions: when they started, how long you focused and how many rounds

None of this leaves your phone, including when you join the leaderboard or when app statistics are sent. Android's cloud backup is switched off for the app. If you use Settings → Your data → Back up, the app writes a file to a place you choose with the system file picker; that file is yours, and the app never uploads it.

3. The leaderboard (optional)

The Ranks tab lets you race friends in a group, and see where you stand among everyone on the leaderboard, by total screen time for the week (lowest first). It stays off until you tap "Continue with Google". Every other part of the app works without an account.

What's collected when you join:

  • Your first name, taken from your Google account. You can change it in Settings → Leaderboard.
  • Your total screen time for the week, as one number for the whole phone, and how many days it covers. It's sent a few times a day while you're signed in.
  • Your groups: the names of groups you start, and which groups you're in.
  • Your Google account email, name and account ID, and a user ID. These are kept by Google's Firebase Authentication so you can sign in. They're never shown to other users.

When you sign in and use the leaderboard, Google's Firebase services also process your IP address and basic device information (such as Android version and phone model) to keep sign-in secure and prevent abuse.

Never collected: which apps you use, how long you spend in any app, when you use your phone, or anything else from section 2.

Who can see it: people in your groups see your first name and weekly total. Everyone on the leaderboard can see your first name and total if you're in the top 50 for a week. Your total also counts, without your name, in everyone's rank and chart. Nobody can see the bottom of the global list.

Where it's kept: on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore in Google's Mumbai, India region, and Firebase Authentication, which may store sign-in records outside India). Google acts as a service provider and doesn't use this data for advertising.

How long: the boards only show this week and last week, and older weekly totals are deleted each time the app syncs. Signing out removes your weekly totals straight away. Your name and group memberships are kept until you leave a group or delete your leaderboard account. If you uninstall without signing out, your last two weekly totals stay stored (no longer shown after two weeks) until you ask me to delete them.

Security: data is encrypted in transit, and the server's access rules only let you change your own name, score and memberships. Nobody can browse the list of groups; a group can only be opened with its code.

4. App statistics, crash reports and news

These help me see which parts of the app help people, fix crashes, and tell you about updates. They use Google's Firebase services and don't need an account.

App statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase). The app counts how its own features are used, for example: that a nudge was shown and whether it was a notification or a full-screen reminder, what you did next (left the app, tapped "I'm done", snoozed or kept going), whether you opened the weekly recap, shared your week, started a focus session or joined the leaderboard, and your answer if the app asks whether it's helping. Firebase also records basic information on its own: a random app instance ID, phone model, Android version, app version, language, approximate country, and when and for how long TakeABreak itself is open.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics). If the app crashes, a report is sent with the error, the app version, phone model, Android version and a random installation ID.

News from the developer (Firebase Cloud Messaging and Remote Config). The app can receive notifications and show a message card on Home. To deliver them, it gets a random notification token and installation ID from Firebase. You can mute these notifications in your phone's settings under TakeABreak → "News from TakeABreak", without affecting nudges.

Never included: which apps you use, your screen time in any app or on the phone, when you use your phone, your limits, your check-in answers, your name or email, or anything else from section 2. The Android advertising ID isn't collected, and nothing is used for ads or shared with advertisers.

How long: Google keeps detailed app statistics for 2 months and crash reports for 90 days. The random IDs are deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

5. What we don't do

  • We don't collect your contacts, location, photos, messages or browsing history. Your name, email and account ID are collected only if you join the leaderboard, as described above.
  • We don't see what you do inside other apps. The app only knows which app is open and for how long, not what's on the screen.
  • We don't use advertising or cross-app tracking tools, and we don't collect the Android advertising ID. App statistics and crash reports (section 4) are used only to improve TakeABreak.
  • We don't sell or share your data with anyone. Google processes the data in sections 3 and 4 only to run these services for us.

6. Optional purchases

"Buy me a coffee" purchases are handled entirely by Google Play. The developer doesn't receive your payment details. The app only learns which support tier you bought, so it can show your supporter badge.

7. Deleting your data and your leaderboard account

Data on your phone: go to Settings → Apps → TakeABreak → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app. Either one removes it all permanently. You can also take back any permission at any time in your phone's settings.

Leaderboard account, in the app: open the ☰ menu on Home → Settings → Leaderboard → Delete my leaderboard account. This removes your name, every weekly total, your group memberships and your sign-in account straight away.

Leaderboard account, without the app: email anupagarwal199@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my TakeABreak account", from the Google account you signed in with. I'll delete the same data within 30 days and reply when it's done.

Only some of it: Settings → Leaderboard → Sign out removes your weekly totals and stops uploads, but keeps your name and groups for when you sign back in. Leaving a group removes you from it.

8. Your rights

You can ask what data I hold about you, correct it (you can also change your name in the app) or delete it, by writing to the email below. Depending on where you live, laws such as India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act or the EU GDPR may give you more rights; I'll honour them.

9. Children

TakeABreak is meant for adults and isn't aimed at children. The leaderboard needs a Google account. If you believe a child has joined the leaderboard, write to me and I'll delete their data.
